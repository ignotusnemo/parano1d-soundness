# RS common agreement and grouped source restoration

The RS-specific common-agreement theorem supplies source restoration for the authorization, History and retained sparse-opening code families. The argument uses Theorem 4.6 of Ben-Sasson, Carmon, Haböck, Kopparty and Saraf, [On Proximity Gaps for Reed-Solomon Codes](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/169/download). It applies directly to Reed-Solomon codes over finite extensions of the production field. Exact checks cover all 421 authenticated finite layers. The result concerns the public-coin code argument. Its use for native adaptive recursive verification requires the coupled all-root compiler.

## Common agreement on the entire chosen set

Let C be the Reed-Solomon code of dimension K on N distinct evaluation points. Write rho=(K-1)/N, let tau be the required agreement fraction and assume tau>sqrt(rho). Fix received words u and v before the challenge. Choose an integer m>=3 satisfying m*tau >= (m+1)*sqrt(rho), and put h=m+1/2. Theorem 4.6 with M=1 gives an exceptional set of challenges of size at most

```text
B(N,rho,tau,m) = N*(2*h^5+3*h*(1-tau)*rho)/(3*rho^(3/2)) + h/sqrt(rho).
```

Outside that set, for every set A of at least tau*N positions on which u+beta*v agrees with a codeword p, there are codewords p0,p1 agreeing with u,v respectively on all of A. The polynomial p0+beta*p1 equals p on A. Since |A|>K-1, polynomial uniqueness gives equality everywhere. The exceptional set is simultaneous over A and p. Thus both the agreement set and the candidate may be chosen after beta. This simultaneous quantifier supplies the restoration property required for candidate switching.

For production challenges uniform on the trace-one support S of size 2^255, the exception probability is at most floor(B)/2^255. Restricting the ambient field to S preserves the exceptional-set cardinality bound. The same argument applies over a finite extension with challenges embedded from the production extension field.

The selected multiplicity may exceed the minimum in the theorem. The displayed bound increases with m. The exact check uses

```text
(m*tau)^2 >= (m+1)^2*(K-1)/N.
```

All quantities in this check are nonnegative. Squaring is therefore equivalent to the required inequality. Together with tau^2>rho, this verifies the theorem's multiplicity condition without numerical square-root rounding.

## Weighted restoration through one fold

Consider an adjacent pair of symbols y0,y1 and its public additive-NTT twiddle omega. Production first computes d=y1+y0 and c=y0+omega*d, then returns c+beta*(c+d). Its inverse takes c,d to y0=c+omega*d and y1=y0+d. The forward and inverse butterflies identify the two child codewords with one parent codeword in the additive-NTT evaluation basis.

Fix parent weights wi in [0,1] before beta. Give a child position the mean of the weights of its two parents. Suppose a child candidate has full agreement set A with weighted mass at least tau. Then |A|>=tau*N for the child domain. Apply the common-agreement statement to u=c and v=c+d. Restore the two parent symbols by the inverse butterfly at every child position. Both restored symbols agree with the received parent above each position of A. Their weighted agreement mass equals that of A because the child weight is the mean of the parent weights. Hence restoration preserves the required weighted mass. The same exceptional set works for every choice of weights and agreement set meeting the stated threshold.

For row batching, pack the surviving rows in a fixed finite extension using a fixed basis. Column Hamming agreement is exactly equality of the packed symbol. Apply the same common-agreement statement to the two packed halves and unpack their restored codewords. This restores the rows on one common set. The 32 initial rows therefore use one packed candidate list. Whenever a component agrees with received base-field values on more positions than its degree, Frobenius conjugation and polynomial uniqueness force its coefficients into the base field.

## Groups and later candidate choices

Expand a group into its consecutive binary folds. Every omitted intermediate word is the deterministic fold of the word and challenge prefix already fixed. Each such word is fixed before its next scalar challenge. A newly chosen committed word starts a new restoration stage. Apply the one-fold statement backwards from the final candidate. The universal agreement-set quantifier permits each restored candidate to be selected after the challenges already exposed. For a group of r folds, the union of the r scalar exception events has probability at most the sum of their bounds. At each individual scalar public-coin move, the corresponding bound is the one for that move.

When a committed middle word interrupts the sequence, use the equality with the preceding folded word as its initial weight indicator. Average these weights over the later fold fibers. Backward restoration preserves that weighted mass and therefore reconnects the restored middle candidate to the earlier word. This yields one source origin through the two committed stages of authorization. Invertible within-leaf normalization preserves equality and the inverse-butterfly argument.

The local round-by-round analysis uses the bound at each scalar move. Mapping those moves and grouped responses to a native oracle execution is part of the all-root compiler. The shared-oracle and charging conditions of that compiler apply to this local theorem as they do to the other local lemmas.
